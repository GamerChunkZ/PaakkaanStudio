Shatteredfield – Privacy Policy

Effective and last updated: 16 November 2026 · Applies to the Shatteredfield game for Android and iOS

In short:
  • Shatteredfield has no account and no sign-in. Your progress, coins and settings are saved on your device only. We do not run any server that receives information about you.
  • The game is free and shows ads: optional rewarded ads you choose to watch (for example double coins or a revive), and a short ad between some runs. Ads come from our advertising partners through Unity LevelPlay.
  • When you first play, we ask your year of birth. We keep only whether you are under 18 or not, never the year. Players under 18 get only child-directed, non-personalised ads and see no tracking or consent prompts.
  • Ads are personalised only if you allow it: on iPhone and iPad through Apple's tracking prompt, and in the EU, UK and Switzerland through a consent form. You can change your choice at any time.
  • Purchases (coin packs and Remove ads) go through Google Play or the App Store. We never see card or bank details.
  • We do not sell your information, and we do not use analytics, location, contacts, camera or microphone.

2. Who the game is for

Shatteredfield is an arcade space shooter for a general audience aged 13 and over. It is not designed for or directed at children under 13. Because players may be younger than 18, the game asks for your year of birth the first time you play (section 4) and treats anyone under 18 as a child for advertising.

3. Information saved on your device

Shatteredfield has no account. The game saves the following on your device only. None of it is sent to us:

4. Age check and privacy choices

Before any ad is requested, the game goes through these steps in this order:

  1. Age check (first launch only). You enter your year of birth on a keypad. Nothing is pre-filled. If you are under 18, the game stops here: you are not shown any tracking or consent prompt, and our ad partners are told to serve only child-directed, non-personalised ads (section 5).
  2. Apple tracking prompt (iPhone and iPad only). Apple's App Tracking Transparency prompt asks whether the game may track you across other companies' apps and websites. It is shown before anything else about privacy. If you choose "Ask App Not to Track", the game does not access your device's advertising identifier, and you get only non-personalised ads.
  3. Consent where the law requires it. The game asks Google's consent tool whether a consent request applies where you are (currently the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland). This check shows nothing on screen.
    • If it applies and tracking is allowed (Android, or you allowed tracking on iOS), you see a consent form provided by Google, where you can accept or refuse personalised advertising and related purposes.
    • If it applies but you asked iOS not to track you, you see no consent form. Instead, a one-time notice confirms that the game does not track you, and only non-personalised ads are shown.
    • If it does not apply where you are, nothing else is shown.
    • If the check cannot be completed (for example when you are offline), nothing is shown and ads are non-personalised until the next time you open the game.

Changing your choices. Where a consent form applies, open Settings ▸ Ad privacy ▸ Change in the game at any time. On iPhone and iPad you can change tracking in Settings ▸ Privacy & Security ▸ Tracking. On Android you can delete or reset your advertising ID in Settings ▸ Privacy ▸ Ads (or Settings ▸ Google ▸ Ads).

5. Advertising

Shatteredfield is free because it shows ads. We use Unity LevelPlay (Unity Technologies, which includes ironSource) to request ads from advertising networks. The networks currently used are Unity Ads and ironSource Ads. If we add another network, we will list it here first.

When an ad is requested or shown, the ad SDKs in the game collect information directly from your device. We do not receive this information ourselves. It can include:

  • your device's advertising identifier (Google Advertising ID on Android; on iOS the IDFA only if you allowed tracking);
  • your IP address, which can indicate your approximate location (country or city, not precise location);
  • device and app information: device model, operating system and version, language, time zone, network type, screen size and the game's version;
  • ad interactions: which ads were shown, whether you watched them to the end or tapped them, and whether a reward was given;
  • the privacy signals the game passes on: whether you are under 18, whether you consented, and whether personal information may be sold or shared.

Our partners use this information to deliver ads, to limit how often you see the same ad, to measure ads, to prevent fraud and, only where you have allowed it, to show ads based on your interests. Partners use the information under their own privacy policies, and some act as independent controllers of it:

Players under 18 are reported to our partners as child-directed users (including under the US Children's Online Privacy Protection Act). Their advertising identifier is not used for personalised ads, no consent is asked for, and they receive only non-personalised ads. Unity Ads also lets you opt out of personalised ads through the privacy icon shown on its ads.

6. How ads are shown

  • Rewarded ads are always your choice. You decide to watch one to get a reward: double coins after a run, a revive once per run, a daily coin gift, or progress toward unlocking a ship. You never have to watch one to play.
  • Ad breaks (full-screen ads) appear only between runs, never during play, never in your first session, and at most once every few runs. A short countdown appears first.
  • Remove ads is a one-time purchase that stops ad breaks. Rewarded ads stay available because you choose them.

7. Purchases

You can buy coin packs and Remove ads. Purchases are made and paid through Google Play or the Apple App Store, which handle payment and hold your payment details under their own privacy policies. We receive only a confirmation of the purchase (product, order or transaction ID, date), and the game records on your device what you bought. We never see your name, card or bank details.

8. Crash reports and the game engine

The game contains no analytics or crash-reporting software of its own. If it crashes, Google Play (through "Android vitals") or Apple (through App Store Connect) may give us a crash report, but only if you have chosen, in your device's settings, to share usage and diagnostics data with app developers. These reports reach us anonymised and contain technical details such as the device model, operating system version and the part of the game that failed. We use them only to fix problems.

The game is built with the Unity engine, which may send limited technical device information (such as device model, operating system, graphics hardware and screen size) to Unity to improve its engine. It does not include your name, contact details or precise location. Unity privacy policy

9. What we do not do

  • We do not ask for your name, email, phone number, photos, contacts or precise location.
  • We do not use usage analytics, social media features or our own tracking.
  • We do not sell your personal information. Where personalised ads are allowed, sharing information with ad partners for that purpose may count as "sharing" under some US state laws; you can stop it as described in sections 4 and 11.
  • We do not use notifications.

10. Where information goes, and how long it is kept

  • On your device: until you delete the game or clear its storage.
  • With advertising partners and Google: processed on their servers, which may be outside your country, including in the United States, and kept for the periods set out in their privacy policies. Where information leaves the EU, the UK or another region with transfer rules, these companies use the legal safeguards those rules require, such as Standard Contractual Clauses.
  • With us: nothing, apart from anonymised crash reports (section 8) and any emails you send us, which we keep only as long as needed to answer them.

11. Your rights, and the laws we follow

Depending on where you live, you have some or all of these rights: to access, correct or delete your information, to receive a copy, to object to or restrict its use, and to withdraw consent at any time. Because the game stores your information only on your device, you can delete it yourself by deleting the game or clearing its storage. To withdraw advertising consent, use Settings ▸ Ad privacy in the game, Apple's tracking setting, or your device's ad settings (section 4). For information held by an advertising partner, you can also contact that partner through the links in section 5. If you need help, email us. We answer within 30 days and never charge for a reasonable request.

  • European Union, United Kingdom and Switzerland – GDPR, UK GDPR and the Swiss FADP. Personalised advertising is based on your consent, given in the consent form and withdrawable at any time. Non-personalised ads are shown on the basis of our legitimate interest in funding a free game. You may complain to your local data protection authority (in the UK, the ICO).
  • United States – COPPA and state privacy laws. Players who say they are under 18 are treated as child-directed users (section 5). Residents of some states (for example California) can opt out of the sale or sharing of personal information and of targeted advertising: on iOS, choose "Ask App Not to Track"; on Android, delete your advertising ID in your device's ad settings. You may also email us.
  • India – the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023. Players under 18 are not tracked, profiled or shown targeted ads. You may raise a grievance with our Grievance Officer (section 1) and then with the Data Protection Board of India.
  • Canada – PIPEDA and Australia – the Privacy Act 1988, and other applicable laws.

If we change how the game handles information, for example by adding cloud save, sign-in or another advertising network, we will update this policy and the date at the top before the change takes effect, and ask for consent again where the law requires it.
